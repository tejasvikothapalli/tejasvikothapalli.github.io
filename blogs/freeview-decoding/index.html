Decoding Scenes from V1 During Natural Vision

Reconstructing static natural scenes from marmoset V1 activity during free viewing.

Authors Tejasvi Kothapalli1, Jacob Yates1
Affiliation 1 UC Berkeley Redwood Center for Theoretical Neuroscience
Published Aug 15, 2026
Tags Decoding, Denoising, Natural Scenes, 1/f2, Free-viewing, V1, Fovea
Abstract

To our knowledge, decoding a full static natural scene from V1 activity during natural vision has never been done. Here, we present an attempt to reconstruct a static scene from V1 activity while a marmoset freely views it. We record neural activity from marmoset foveal V1. Due to retinotopy and the recording location, all recorded cells respond to a ~0.5° × 0.5° region of visual space near the center of gaze. At every fixation, a decoder predicts this gaze-contingent patch from the population’s neural activity. Eye position places each decoded patch in scene coordinates; patches accumulated across fixations and sessions are then combined with a 1/f prior to reconstruct the full scene. The interactive visualization below shows both patch-level decoding fidelity and the resulting full-scene reconstruction. Every reconstruction is fully held out: the decoder was never trained on the scene it reconstructs.
